# Relay voice-over: the natural script (about 65 seconds)

Say it the way you would tell a friend: warm, unhurried, a smile in the voice. The pauses between lines are part of the pacing; the music and the visuals carry them. Each line is its own clip, so you can record them one at a time.

**0:00  Stakes: no signal, the request, one tap**

> **L1**  A disaster has knocked out the network, and a woman is having a heart attack. So you open Relay, tap once, and it reaches out through the phones around you.
>

**0:12  The spark hops across the city**

> **L2**  Every phone with Relay becomes part of the network. It hops, phone to phone.
>

**0:17  The gap, then a neighbour shares the app**

> **L3**  Then, a gap. Nobody in the middle has Relay. So a neighbour shares the app, and the link is made.
>

**0:24  It crosses the city to the doctor**

> **L4**  Now it crosses the city, to a doctor who can help. She sees it, and she answers.
>

**0:32  The offer comes home, she acknowledges, help is coming**

> **L5a**  And the answer finds its way back.
>
> **L5b**  She acknowledges the offer.
>
> **L5c**  Someone is coming.
>

**0:44  A missing person: photo, eyes, a sighting**

> **L6a**  And if someone you love is missing, add their photo.
>
> **L6b**  Every phone becomes a pair of eyes,
>
> **L6c**  and a sighting comes back.
>

**0:56  Community updates**

> **L7**  Community updates reach everyone, too.
>

**0:59  Hope, and the hand-off to your live demo**

> **L8**  When the towers go quiet, neighbours become the network.
>
> **L9**  Now, watch it on real phones.
>

**Delivery notes**
- L1: serious on "heart attack", then lift gently on "tap once".
- L5b and L5c are short on purpose. Let "Someone is coming." land quietly.
- L6c: say "a sighting", never "a rescue"; the app labels every response unverified, and so does the video.
- L9: an invitation to your live demo, friendly and a touch brighter, not a sign-off.
